Hockey moves too fast for a score that only updates when you remember to check. The **hockey.scores** widget, provided by the **[hockey](https://github.com/JamesAwesome/led-ticker-plugins/tree/main/plugins/hockey)** package (`type = "hockey.scores"`), holds a scoreboard per game on your wall: the score, the period and a running clock, shots on goal, the power play and its time left, an empty net, finals, and the next scheduled game. When a team you follow scores, its card plays a goal celebration the next time it comes up in the rotation.

This page is for sign owners configuring the widget in TOML. No API key and no account are needed.

**Requires led-ticker-core >= 4.30.**

<Aside type="caution" title="An unofficial data feed">
  Scores come from the NHL's public web API (`api-web.nhle.com`). It is free and needs no key, but
  it is **unofficial and undocumented** — the NHL can change its shape at any time. The plugin
  parses it defensively: a bad or missing payload keeps the last good scores on the panel with a
  `STALE` label rather than crashing the sign. The plugin is not affiliated with or endorsed by the
  NHL.
</Aside>

## What you'll need

- A working [led-ticker](https://github.com/JamesAwesome/led-ticker) install, core **4.30 or newer**.
- The plugin: add `led-ticker-hockey` to your sign's `config/requirements-plugins.txt` and restart. See [using plugins](/plugins/) for the full install path.
- Internet access on the Pi. Nothing else — no key, no sign-up.

Run the config commands wherever your sign's `config/` directory lives — on the Pi itself for a deployed sign.

## The smallest config that works

```toml
[[playlist.section]]
mode = "slideshow"
hold_time = 6

[[playlist.section.widget]]
type = "hockey.scores"
teams = ["PHI", "NYI"]
demo = true
```

That is the whole widget block from `docs/site/demos-pinned/hockey-scoreboard-longboi.toml`, the file that rendered the GIF above. Delete `demo = true` and the same block follows the Flyers and the Islanders live.

`teams` takes NHL three-letter codes, case-insensitive, and is required in the default mode. `mode = "slideshow"` is the right section mode: one game per visit, held, then a transition to the next. If you want start times in your own timezone, add `timezone = "America/Chicago"` (any IANA name; the default is `America/New_York`).

## Three ways to pick games

`mode` decides which games get a card. Each game is one card in the rotation, and two tracked teams playing each other share one card.

- **`teams` (default)** — only games involving your `teams`, in config order. For each team: its live game; else a final still inside `final_hold_hours`; else a game later today; else its next scheduled game, such as Friday's game or the season opener.
- **`league`** — every NHL game today: your teams first, then other live games, then finals inside the hold, then games later today by start time. Capped at `max_games`.
- **`live`** — only games in progress, your teams first, capped at `max_games`. With nothing on, the widget has no cards and drops out of the rotation until a game starts.

In `league` and `live` modes, `teams` is optional: it sets who comes first and which goals celebrate.

## Layouts, one held board per sign shape

`layout = "auto"` (the default) picks a held scoreboard on all three sign shapes. A held board reads better than a crawl, even on a 16-row strip.

### Longboi (512×64)

A full scoreboard, shown at the top of this page. The away team fills the left third (chip, large abbreviation, `SOG`, huge score), the home team mirrors it on the right, and the centre holds the period, a large clock, and the **PP** badge with `TEAM m:ss` beneath it. Next-game cards add the team nicknames.

### Bigsign (256×64)

Two team rows with a dotted divider — chip, abbreviation, large score, `SOG nn` — with an amber **PP** badge on the power-play team's row. The right column shows the period, the clock and `PP m:ss`.

### On every board

- **Intermission:** the clock reads `1ST INT` / `2ND INT` with the countdown.
- **Empty net:** `EN` marks the team that pulled its goalie.
- **Final:** `FINAL`, marked `OT` or `SO` when it went past regulation, the winner's score green and the loser's dimmed.
- **Next game:** both chips, `@`, the day and date, the time and the arena.

Game clocks, power-play time and intermission countdowns tick every second between polls; the board counts down from the last poll and stops at `0:00`. Team abbreviations shrink to fit (both rows always share one size). Nothing on the boards is user-tunable — the palette is fixed.

### The scrolling ticker

Set `layout = "ticker"` for one scrolling line per game, in the same wording as the boards. Use `mode = "ticker"` on the section, since this layout scrolls rather than holds. At scale 1 the clock text refreshes with each poll rather than every second; the boards and the scale > 1 crawl tick every second.

## Reading the colors

| Color       | Means                                                             |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Team colors | The chip beside each abbreviation, and the abbreviation itself    |
| White       | Scores and the game clock                                         |
| Amber       | The period, the power play (`PP` badge and time), next-game dates |
| Cyan        | Shots on goal                                                     |
| Green       | The winner's score on a final                                     |
| Dim gray    | The loser's score on a final                                      |
| Dark gray   | `STALE`, the arena, and labels such as the smallsign's `SOG`      |

## Goal celebration

When a team in your `teams` list scores, that game's card plays a 4-second celebration **the next time its turn comes around**. It never interrupts whatever else is on the panel. You get three quick flashes in the scoring team's color with `GOAL` knocked out, then the new score, the scorer with their season goal count (`KONECNY (5)`), the assists, and the period and time of the goal. Then the normal board for the rest of the hold.

- **The hold stretches to fit.** While a celebration is pending the card asks for 4 seconds per queued goal plus the larger of the widget's `hold_time` and 4 seconds for the board afterwards; the engine still uses the section's `hold_time` if that is longer. With `hold_time = 6` on the section, a card with one goal to celebrate holds for 8 seconds. You don't need to raise `hold_time` for it.
- **Several goals queue.** Goals scored between turns celebrate in order, up to 3 per turn; older ones are dropped.
- **Only your teams flash.** In `league` and `live` modes a goal by a team you don't track highlights the score briefly and fades back to white. With an empty `teams` list nothing celebrates.
- **Each goal celebrates once.** A celebration counts as done only once it actually reached the panel, and later rotations don't replay it.
- **Corrections don't re-celebrate.** When the NHL changes a goal's scorer or assists, the queued celebration shows the corrected name; it never plays twice. An overturned goal quietly corrects the score and drops any queued celebration for it.
- **Boot is silent.** Goals already in the feed when the widget first sees a game — after a boot, restart or [hot reload](/concepts/hot-reload/), or a game found mid-period — don't celebrate.

During a transition into or out of the card, the board is shown; the celebration plays once the card is on screen.

## How often does it poll the NHL?

Every `hockey.scores` widget in one process shares a single fetcher, so several widgets don't multiply requests.

- **Every 15 seconds** while any game you're showing is live — faster if a widget sets a lower `update_interval` (floored at 10).
- **Every 5 minutes** when games are scheduled today but haven't started.
- **About hourly** otherwise.
- **At most every 6 hours** for the season schedule, used to find a team's next game in the offseason.

The power-play and empty-net lookup is only made for live games that are on screen. A failed request keeps the last good games, marks every card `STALE`, and backs off — starting at twice the interval, doubling, capped at four hours.

## Try it in demo mode

`demo = true` renders four fixture games between PHI and NYI with no network: a live game on an NYI power play with a PHI goal, a first intermission, an OT final, and next Friday's game. The live game's goal celebrates on its first turn and again every third turn after, so a looping demo keeps showing it. Keep `teams` set — `demo = true` works without it, but with no tracked team the goal only highlights the score instead of celebrating.

It is the quickest way to check the layout on your panel, and it is what every GIF on this page shows.

## If it doesn't work

**`led-ticker validate` says `unknown code`.** A team code isn't one of the 32 NHL abbreviations. The message suggests the closest match — `PHL` gets `Did you mean 'PHI'?` — so swap in the suggested code.

**The board says `STALE`.** A fetch failed or the feed returned something the plugin couldn't parse, so it's showing the last good scores. The logs carry the reason; the label clears on the next good poll. If it never clears, the NHL may have changed the feed.

**The section went dark.** Nothing qualified for a card: `live` mode with no game on, or a `teams` team with nothing scheduled — the offseason. A section holding only this widget has nothing to show then, and the sign idles blank (see [scheduling](/concepts/scheduling/) for how core handles an empty section). Put the widget in a section with other content to keep the panel lit.

**The sign showed a goal before my TV did.** Expected. The feed is near real-time and broadcasts run on a delay, so the panel can be ahead of the screen. There is no spoiler delay.

**There's no `PP` or `EN` on a live game.** The power-play and empty-net fields come from a part of the feed that has not yet been checked against a live game. If the NHL shapes them differently, the board leaves them off and everything else keeps working.

**My team scored but nothing celebrated.** Check that the team is in `teams`, and remember the first sight of a game after a boot or reload is silent by design.
